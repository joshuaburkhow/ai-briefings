Astro's Creator Ships Flue 2, a React-Style Harness for AI Agents

  • Fred Schott, creator of the Astro web framework (acquired by Cloudflare in January), shipped Flue 2, the framework's first stable release, built around React-style “Agent Hooks” that let a JavaScript function represent an agent and re-render itself before every model call.AINews
  • Flue 2 ships 16 built-in hooks, including useSkill(), useTool() and useSubagent(), so agents can attach or drop capabilities dynamically at runtime instead of being fully configured in advance — Schott says this is needed for “real support bots, real triage bots” that must adapt mid-conversation.AINews
  • The framework is built on Pi, an open-source minimal harness, with Schott arguing “there is no agent without a harness” — positioning Flue against Vercel's similarly-built eve, while staying explicitly host-portable rather than tied to Cloudflare, his own employer.AINews
  • Hosted agents in Flue 2 are now built with Vite, and LangChain's new Managed Deep Agents offering was cited as evidence that hosted-agent platforms are becoming a competitive category, though Schott says a managed product isn't currently on Flue's roadmap.AINews
Why it matters

Agent frameworks are picking their opinions before the category has even settled. Flue's bet is that the harness — not the model — is the part worth standardizing, and its React-style hooks try to give agent development the same maturity curve web frameworks went through a decade ago. Worth watching alongside DeepSeek's newly open-sourced harness from two days ago: two credible, differently-opinionated harnesses shipped in the same week.

The Team Behind 'Smallville' Built a $2 Billion Startup to Simulate All 8 Billion of Us

  • Simile AI, grown out of Stanford researcher Joon Sung Park's 2023 “Generative Agents” paper (the viral Smallville simulation of 25 AI townspeople), has reached a $2 billion valuation in under six months.Turing Post
  • The company's stated ambition is a “CERN of human society”: simulating bank runs, climate cooperation and democratic collapse, with CEO Park describing a future foundational simulation that could cost $100 million and take months to run.Turing Post
  • The research path to get here included a 2025 study grounding agents in two-hour interviews with 1,052 real Americans, and SocSci210, a 2.9-million-response dataset used to fine-tune a 14B-parameter Qwen model that beat GPT-4o by 13% at reproducing unseen social-science results.Turing Post
  • Co-founders Percy Liang and Michael Bernstein, Park's Stanford advisors, bring foundation-model and human-computer-interaction expertise; CVS Health and Gallup are named as early users of the resulting enterprise platform.Turing Post
Why it matters

Synthetic humans are becoming a funded product category, not a research demo. A $2B valuation on a six-month-old company says investors think simulated populations can substitute for expensive market research, clinical trials and policy testing. The open question the piece raises but doesn't settle is whether persona agents are accurate enough to replace the real surveys and experiments they're pitched against.

Researchers Went From Zoom Bug to Working Exploit in Under 24 Hours Using Public AI Models

  • Security researchers at A Security disclosed “Zoomsday,” a vulnerability chain in Zoom's annotation feature that let another meeting participant execute code on a victim's device with no interaction and no visible warning, affecting Zoom Workplace on Windows, macOS, Linux, Android and iOS.Weekly Dose
  • The researchers say they went from vulnerability discovery to a working cross-platform exploit in under 24 hours using fewer than 20 prompts to publicly available AI models — evidence that AI can compress code analysis and exploit construction into a dramatically shorter workflow.Weekly Dose
  • Zoom released fixes through coordinated disclosure; affected versions are those before 7.0.6, or 7.1.5 for fast-track users.Weekly Dose
  • The disclosure lands the same week OpenAI split its own cyber-capable model into access tiers (GPT-5.6-Cyber's Daybreak Blue/Red) specifically to control who gets vulnerability-research-grade AI.Weekly Dose
Why it matters

The gap between finding a bug and weaponizing it is shrinking faster than patch cycles are. A sub-20-prompt, sub-24-hour exploit timeline means the old assumption — that attackers need weeks of specialized research — no longer holds. Security teams should treat that compression as the baseline threat model now, not an edge case.

Anthropic Names Its Watermark 'SynthID-Text' and Promises a Detector; Google Lets Users Turn Theirs Off

  • Anthropic confirmed the invisible text watermarks it's adding to Claude output use Google's SynthID-Text method, which subtly steers word choice without adding hidden characters, changing quality, or raising cost; the company plans to apply it globally and says a watermark-detection API is coming soon.AI Entrepreneurs
  • Separately, Google added a setting letting users toggle visible watermarks on or off across AI-generated images, video and music inside Gemini Apps, though the control isn't available on work/school accounts and requires an AI Ultra subscription in India, South Korea and Vietnam.AI Entrepreneurs
  • Turning off Gemini's visible watermark does not remove the invisible SynthID watermark or C2PA Content Credentials metadata, which stay embedded specifically so platforms can still identify AI-generated media after the visible mark is gone.Brain Buzz
  • Both moves are framed as compliance with the EU AI Act's transparency requirements, which is pushing watermarking from a research feature into a configurable, cross-company product standard.Brain Buzz
Why it matters

Watermarking is graduating from 'we do this' to 'here's exactly how, and here's your dial.' A named method, a coming detector API, and a user-facing on/off toggle are the difference between a compliance checkbox and a real provenance system. Anyone building on Claude or Gemini output should expect watermark detection to become a standard verification step, not an edge case.

Concepts to know

Agent Hooks
AINews

React-style functions that let an AI agent manage its own state, listen to lifecycle events, and attach tools or skills dynamically at runtime, rather than being fully configured in advance. Flue 2 popularized the pattern with 16 built-in hooks like useSkill() and useTool().

Meta-harness
AINews

A proposed layer offering one API for coordinating across multiple different agent harnesses or frameworks (examples cited: Databricks' Omnigent, the self-improving Exo harness). Still an unsettled term this early in agent-framework development, per Flue's creator.

SynthID-Text
AI Entrepreneurs

Google's watermarking method, now used by Anthropic for Claude's text output, that subtly biases word choice during generation so the text can later be verified as AI-written, without altering output quality, cost, or identifying the user.

Organizational physics
AI Corner

Investor Sarah Guo's framing for why startups can out-compete AI labs on specific products: a big company has exactly one “A-team,” so most of its own product lines are staffed by a funded-but-unloved B-team, leaving room for focused startups to win on execution.
